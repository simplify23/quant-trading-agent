\title{Admission Gates as a Quality-Control Layer\\
for LLM-Driven Alpha Mining\\
\large An Injected-Truth Validation on 795 CSI~800 Constituents}

\author{Anonymous}
\date{October 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
LLM-driven frameworks such as AlphaSeek and QuantaAlpha excel at mining alpha
factors, but none reports the probability that a discovered factor is a false
discovery. We present a set of \textbf{admission gates}---statistical criteria
that must be satisfied before a mined conclusion is adopted---that operates as
a \emph{quality-control layer on top of} any alpha mining framework. We
validate the gates through an \textbf{injected-truth protocol} with a
\textbf{random-admission control} on 795 CSI~800 constituents (2019--2026).
Applied to a 72-configuration grid search (simulating the output of an alpha
mining framework), our gates reduce the adoption rate from 100\% to 5\%,
eliminate all false discoveries in the weak-signal regime, and correctly
identify that the training-best configuration (Calmar 1.04) fails
out-of-sample (Calmar 0.65). We further discover a \textbf{temporal
aggregation mismatch}: per-trade signal improvements of $+7.7$pp do not
transfer to portfolio-level performance ($+7.0\%$ vs.\ buy-and-hold
$+20.3\%$) because signals cluster near rebounds while portfolios hold
through troughs. A 30-trial Bayesian optimisation (TPE) confirms the finding
with 58\% fewer trials. We position admission gates as a complementary layer:
AlphaSeek mines, we validate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

LLM-driven frameworks are transforming alpha factor discovery. AlphaSeek
\citep{alphaseek} mutates mining trajectories and reports CSI300 ARR of
8.28\%; QuantaAlpha \citep{quantaalpha} evolves trajectories via
self-reflection with ARR 27.75\%; FactorMiner \citep{factorMiner} achieves
IC 8.25\% on CSI500; AlphaAgent \citep{alphaagent} constrains generation by
syntactic originality. These systems are powerful. But they share a blind
spot: \emph{none reports the probability that a discovered factor is a false
discovery}.

The replication crisis in finance is well documented: \citet{mcleanpontiff}
showed returns to published predictors decline 58\% post-publication;
\citet{harveyliuzhu} showed the appropriate $t$-threshold rises with the
number of factors tried; \citet{houxuezhang} found 82\% of published
anomalies fail multiple-testing hurdles. These results imply that faster
mining without better validation produces \emph{more false discoveries}, not
fewer.

We propose admission gates as a \textbf{quality-control layer} that operates
on top of any alpha mining framework. The gates do not mine---they
\emph{validate}. Given a set of candidate signals produced by AlphaSeek,
QuantaAlpha, or any other framework, our gates determine which conclusions
are statistically trustworthy enough to deploy.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Injected-truth protocol with random-admission control}: We
  validate admission gates by injecting known-$\alpha$ signals, using a
  control that adopts at the same rate. Gates eliminate false discoveries in
  the weak-signal regime (0\% false positive rate).
\item \textbf{Temporal aggregation mismatch}: We discover that per-signal
  improvements of $+7.7$pp do not transfer to portfolio-level returns
  ($+7.0\%$ vs.\ $+20.3\%$ buy-and-hold), because signals cluster near
  rebounds while portfolios hold through troughs.
\item \textbf{BO underperforms grid for threshold signals}: TPE with 30
  trials achieves 93.6\% of the grid optimum with less overfitting, but
  cannot find the grid optimum for discontinuous response surfaces.
\item \textbf{M-series rules}: We distil 12 rules from master traders
  (Simons, Thorp, de Prado, top Chinese funds) into executable checks, each
  mapped to a concrete failure case from live operation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Method: Admission Gates}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Architecture}

Our framework operates as a quality-control layer on top of any alpha mining
system. The mining framework (AlphaSeek, QuantaAlpha, or a simple grid search)
produces $N$ candidate configurations. Our gates evaluate each candidate and
decide: adopt, reject, or pending.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Admission gate criteria.}
\label{tab:gates}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lp{4.5cm}p{5cm}@{}}
\toprule
Gate & Criterion & Failure mode addressed \\
\midrule
G1 & Anchor self-check & Broken pipeline produces garbage \\
G2 & Candidate $>$ baseline on primary metric & Adoption without improvement \\
G3 & Risk not worse than baseline & Return at cost of higher drawdown \\
G4 & Affected sample $\ge 8$ & Noise mistaken for signal \\
G6 & Continuous improvement region $\ge 4$ cells & Single lucky parameter point \\
G7 & Effect not concentrated in one year & Regime-specific artefact \\
G8 & No-lookahead version also passes & Subtle future information leakage \\
G9a & DSR $\ge 0.95$ after trial correction & Multiple-testing false discovery \\
G9b & PBO $\le 0.5$ & Backtest overfitting \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Injected-truth protocol}

We validate the gates by injecting signals with known ground truth. A
random-admission control adopts at the same rate as the gate. If the gate
beats this control, it carries information rather than merely raising a
threshold.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}

All experiments use 795 CSI~800 constituents (2019--2026), Tushare daily
data. Forward returns: $T{+}1$ open to $T{+}1{+}h$ close, 26bp round-trip.
Training: 2019--2023. Validation: 2024--2026.

\subsection{The 72-configuration mining experiment}
\label{sec:grid}

We simulate an alpha mining framework by running a 72-configuration grid
search over a deep-dip signal family:

\begin{itemize}
\item Deep-dip threshold: $r_1 \in \{-6\%, -8\%\}$
\item Book-to-price window: $w \in \{250, 500, 1000\}$ days
\item Book-to-price gate: $g \in \{30\%, 40\%, 60\%, \infty\}$
\item Holding period: $h \in \{10, 20, 40\}$ days
\end{itemize}

Each configuration produces a signal with forward returns on 795 stocks.
This is analogous to AlphaSeek's mined factors: each cell is a ``discovered
alpha'' with in-sample statistics.

\subsection{Gate filtering results}
\label{sec:gatetest}

\textbf{Claim: gates correctly identify the training-best as a false discovery.}

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Gate filtering of 72 mined configurations.}
\label{tab:gatefilter}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
 & Without gates & With gates \\
\midrule
Candidates evaluated & 72 & 72 \\
Candidates adopted & 72 (100\%) & 4 (5.6\%) \\
Training-best Calmar & 1.040 & 0.973 \\
Validation-best Calmar & 0.648 & \textbf{0.667} \\
Training $\to$ Valid.\ decay & 38\% & \textbf{3\%} \\
False positives in adopted set & High & \textbf{Low} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{What to observe}: without gates, the mining framework would adopt
the training-best (Calmar 1.040), which decays 38\% in validation. With
gates, only 4 of 72 configurations pass, and the best surviving
configuration has \emph{higher} validation Calmar (0.667 vs.\ 0.648) with
only 3\% decay. The gates act as a \emph{regulariser}: they sacrifice
in-sample peak performance for out-of-sample stability.

\label{sec:mismatch}

The per-signal edge is genuine ($+7.68$pp, 68\% win rate, zero noise-band

through every day of the trough.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Admission gates as a stacking layer}

Our framework is not a competitor to AlphaSeek or QuantaAlpha. It is a
\emph{quality-control layer} that operates on top of them. The architecture
is:

\begin{verbatim}
  AlphaSeek / QuantaAlpha    →  mines N candidate factors
           ↓
  Our Admission Gates        →  filters to k << N trustworthy conclusions
           ↓
  Portfolio Construction     →  deploys only gated conclusions
\end{verbatim}

This is analogous to de Prado's meta-labeling: a secondary model that
decides whether to take a trade suggested by a primary model. Our gates are
the secondary model for the \emph{research conclusion}, not the trade.

\subsection{Why no existing framework reports FDR}

Table~\ref{tab:competitive} shows that no alpha mining framework reports
false discovery rate. This is not because FDR is unimportant---it is because
measuring FDR requires \emph{ground truth}, which is unavailable when mining
real factors. Our injected-truth protocol solves this by creating synthetic
signals with known properties.

The paper itself demonstrates the self-learning it describes.
Table~\ref{tab:recursion} traces the paper's own improvement iterations,
each discovering a flaw in the previous version---mirroring the framework's
L3 protocol self-correction.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Survivorship bias}: current CSI~800 constituents only.
\item \textbf{Autocorrelation clustering}: effective independent trials far
  smaller than nominal 8{,}795.
\item \textbf{Threshold signals resist BO}: discontinuous response surfaces
  at trigger boundaries make TPE/GP less reliable than grid search.
\item \textbf{Cost model simplified}: 26bp does not capture market impact or
  limit-up unavailability.
\item \textbf{Single market}: all experiments on Chinese A-shares.
\item \textbf{The agent cannot predict regime changes}: it discovers
  methodological flaws but cannot foresee exogenous market events.
\item \textbf{Rule inflation risk}: each self-learning round adds rules,
  increasing trial count and DSR penalty. Quarterly reviews are needed.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

We presented admission gates as a quality-control layer for LLM-driven alpha
mining. Validated through an injected-truth protocol with random-admission
control, the gates reduce the adoption rate from 100\% to 5.6\%, eliminate
false discoveries in the weak-signal regime, and correctly identify that the
training-best configuration from a 72-cell search fails out-of-sample.

The temporal aggregation mismatch---discovered by the agent through
self-experimentation---demonstrates that per-signal improvements do not
transfer to portfolio-level returns. This finding is invisible without the
self-learning loop and would lead to significant capital losses if deployed
without the gate system.

We position admission gates as a complementary layer: AlphaSeek mines,
we validate. The two systems stack to produce \emph{both} more alpha
\emph{and} fewer false discoveries.
